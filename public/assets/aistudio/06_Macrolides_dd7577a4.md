**Lecture slides covered:** Macrolides (action, uses, agents) → Macrolides side effects (GI, hearing, phlebitis, liver, pregnancy category)

---

## PART A: THEORY

### A1. What are macrolides?
- **Mechanism:** bind the **50S ribosomal subunit** and block bacterial protein synthesis → **bacteriostatic** (slide). *(At high concentrations they can kill some germs, but in exams call them bacteriostatic.)*
- **Name clue:** drug names ending in **-thromycin** (erythromycin, clarithromycin, azithromycin, roxithromycin).
- **Uses (slide):** respiratory tract infections (pharyngitis, sinusitis, bronchitis), genital, gastrointestinal and skin infections.
- **Extra uses (frequently tested):**
  - **Atypical pneumonia** (*Mycoplasma*, *Chlamydophila*, *Legionella*): add to amoxicillin in pneumonia.
  - **Alternative for penicillin-allergic** patients.
  - **Chlamydia, trachoma, typhoid, cholera** (azithromycin; azithromycin or erythromycin is the cholera option in breastfeeding mothers and children).
  - **H. pylori eradication** (clarithromycin).
  - Whooping cough, diphtheria carriers.

### A2. Agents (slide) and what is special about each
| Drug (brand) | Special points |
|---|---|
| **Erythromycin (Erythrocin)** | The original. Short half-life (4 times daily). Most GI upset. Strong CYP450 inhibitor. Stimulates gut movement (so cramps/diarrhoea) |
| **Clarithromycin (Klacid)** | Twice daily. Part of **H. pylori triple therapy**. Strong CYP450 inhibitor. Pregnancy category C |
| **Azithromycin (Zithromax)** | **Long half-life**, so **once daily for 3 to 5 days** (even a single dose for chlamydia). Fewer drug interactions. Pregnancy category B |
| **Roxithromycin (Roxicin)** | Twice daily, similar to erythromycin, fewer GI effects |

### A3. Side effects (slide + extras)
1. **Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal cramps** (commonest).
2. **Temporary hearing impairment** (slide), mainly with high-dose IV erythromycin.
3. **Oral erythromycin irritates the stomach; IV erythromycin causes severe phlebitis** → dilute well, infuse slowly, rotate sites.
4. **Liver:** use **with caution in liver disease** (erythromycin estolate can cause cholestatic jaundice).
5. **QT prolongation** (palpitations, fainting, dangerous arrhythmias).
6. **Drug interactions:** erythromycin and clarithromycin **inhibit liver enzymes (CYP3A4)** → raise levels of **warfarin, carbamazepine, theophylline/aminophylline, digoxin, statins**.
7. **Pregnancy (slide):** **azithromycin and erythromycin = category B** (acceptable); **clarithromycin = category C** (avoid unless needed).

> **Rationale:** Because they sit on the ribosome (not on the cell wall) macrolides work on **atypical bacteria that have no ordinary wall**, which beta-lactams cannot reach. That is why they are the **partner** in pneumonia regimens.

### A4. Nursing care
- Give erythromycin **with or after food** if it upsets the stomach (enteric-coated forms); azithromycin capsules **1 hour before or 2 hours after food**.
- Teach: finish the course; report palpitations, yellow eyes, severe diarrhoea.
- Check the **medication list** for interacting drugs (warfarin, theophylline, statins).

## PART D: ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 → D. Macrolide.** **-thromycin** = macrolide. Azithromycin: once daily for 3 to 5 days (long half-life); used for respiratory infections, atypical pneumonia, chlamydia, typhoid, trachoma; side effects: GI upset, QT prolongation.
- A = cephalosporin (cef-). B = aminoglycoside (gentamicin, amikacin, **-mycin** spelled with "my"). C = fluoroquinolone (**-floxacin**).

**Q2 → D. Macrolide.** Clarithromycin: twice daily, used for chest infections and **H. pylori**; inhibits liver enzymes → raises levels of many drugs.
- A = sulfamethoxazole. B = gentamicin. C = doxycycline.
- *Spelling trap:* **"-mycin"** (with -my-) usually = aminoglycoside (gentamicin, streptomycin, neomycin, kanamycin) **except** erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin (macrolides, **-thromycin**) and clindamycin/lincomycin (lincosamides). Memorise: **gent-/strept-/kana-/neo-/tobra-/amika-** = aminoglycosides.

**Q3 → D. Macrolide.** Same logic as Q1.
- A tetracycline (acts on 30S). B cephalosporin (cell wall). C glycopeptide (vancomycin).

**Q4 → B. Amoxicillin, Clarithromycin, Lansoprazole.**
- **Standard triple therapy = 1 PPI + 2 antibiotics** (amoxicillin + clarithromycin, or metronidazole if penicillin-allergic), twice daily for 7 to 14 days.
- *Why each part:* the **PPI** lowers acid so the antibiotics work and the ulcer heals; the **two antibiotics** kill *H. pylori* (two to prevent resistance).
- *Wrong:* A has **two PPIs** and one antibiotic. C has a PPI + H2 blocker + one antibiotic. D has a PPI + H2 blocker + one antibiotic (tinidazole).
- *Counselling:* finish the course, **avoid alcohol with metronidazole/tinidazole** (disulfiram-like reaction), expect metallic taste and diarrhoea.

---

## PART E: HOW TO ALWAYS GET THESE RIGHT
1. **"-thromycin" → macrolide.** (azi-, clari-, ery-, roxi-)
2. **H. pylori kit = PPI + 2 antibiotics.** Count the antibiotics.
3. If the question asks for **atypical pneumonia** or **penicillin-allergic respiratory infection** → macrolide.
4. **Macrolide + other drug** questions: think **CYP3A4 inhibition** → toxicity of warfarin, theophylline, carbamazepine, digoxin, statins.
5. Do **not** confuse with **aminoglycosides** (oto/nephrotoxic, IV only).

### Memory aids
- **"ERY-thro-mycin = ERRand (GI upset) boy; AZI = Always Zips (once daily); CLARI = Clearly inhibits liver enzymes (and treats H. pylori)."**
- **"Macros hit the 50 (S)."**
- **"Triple for the bug: PPI + Amox + Clari."**

## PART G: SECTION SUMMARY
- Macrolides: **50S, bacteriostatic**, "-thromycin".
- Uses: respiratory, atypical pneumonia, penicillin-allergy alternative, chlamydia, typhoid, cholera, **H. pylori**.
- Side effects: GI upset, **phlebitis (IV)**, transient hearing loss, QT, liver caution, **CYP450 inhibition**.
- Pregnancy: azithromycin and erythromycin B; clarithromycin C.
- **H. pylori kit = PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin.**
