# SECTION 7: LINCOSAMIDES (Clindamycin, Lincomycin) and Antibiotic-Associated Diarrhoea

**Lecture slides covered:** Lincosamides (mechanism, uses) → Toxic shock syndrome → Pseudomembranous colitis (C. difficile)
**Linked past-paper cluster:** antibiotic-induced diarrhoea, loperamide, probiotics (Enterogermina)

---

## PART A: THEORY

### A1. What are lincosamides?
- **Examples:** **lincomycin**, **clindamycin** (clindamycin is stronger and more used).
- **Mechanism (slide):** stop bacterial replication by **blocking protein synthesis at the 50S ribosomal subunit** → **bacteriostatic**.
- **Uses (slide):**
  - **Staphylococci and streptococci** (skin infections, bone and joint infections, dental infections; good **bone penetration**).
  - ***Bacteroides fragilis* and other anaerobes** (abdominal and pelvic infections, aspiration pneumonia).
  - **Toxic Shock Syndrome** (slide): clindamycin **switches off toxin production** by the bacteria, so it is added to the beta-lactam.
  - Extras: first-trimester **malaria in pregnancy** (quinine + clindamycin), toxoplasmosis.
- **Not active against:** Gram-negative aerobes (*E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*), MRSA varies, enterococci.

### A2. The big warning: pseudomembranous colitis (slide)
- Lincosamides (especially **clindamycin**) are among the antibiotics **most associated** with **pseudomembranous colitis caused by *Clostridioides difficile*** (C. diff).
- **Why it happens (rationale):** antibiotics kill the **normal gut bacteria**, so resistant *C. difficile* spores multiply and release **toxins** that damage the colon wall → **watery or bloody diarrhoea, abdominal pain, fever**, and can progress to **toxic megacolon**.
- **When:** during or up to **weeks after** the antibiotic.
- **Management (nurse view):**
  1. **Suspect it** in any patient with new, persistent diarrhoea after antibiotics. Report and send stool test.
  2. **Stop** the causative antibiotic if possible.
  3. **Do NOT give loperamide/antimotility drugs** (they **trap the toxin** and worsen colitis).
  4. Treatment: **oral vancomycin, fidaxomicin or metronidazole** (per local protocol).
  5. **Infection control:** contact precautions, **soap-and-water** handwashing (**alcohol rub does not kill spores**), dedicated equipment, isolation.
  6. Rehydrate.

### A3. Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea in general (mild form)
- Caused by loss of normal flora, not always *C. difficile*.
- **Management:** oral rehydration, **probiotics** (e.g., *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, *Bacillus clausii* = **Enterogermina**) to **replenish normal flora**, review need for the antibiotic.
- **Avoid loperamide** in infective, bloody or antibiotic-associated diarrhoea.
- **Loperamide facts (tested):** opioid-receptor agonist in the gut, slows motility. Stop if diarrhoea persists **48 hours**. Not for children under 2 (CNS/respiratory depression). Not for dysentery/bloody diarrhoea.

## PART D: ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 → C. Lactobacillus acidophilus.** Antibiotics wipe out normal gut flora; probiotics **restore** it.
- A charcoal: for poisons. B bismuth subsalicylate: traveller's diarrhoea (contains salicylate). D **loperamide: avoided** because it can trap *C. difficile* toxin.
- *Pattern:* "antibiotic-associated" + "replenish" → **probiotic**.

**Q2 → D. Replenish normal flora.** Enterogermina = spores of *Bacillus clausii* (a probiotic). Mainstay of diarrhoea treatment is still **ORS + zinc (children)**.
- A = antibiotics. B = loperamide. C = kaolin/adsorbents.

**Q3 → C. Replenish normal flora.** Same drug. **Zinc** (not Enterogermina) helps repair the intestinal lining (option D).

**Q4 → B.** Loperamide is **stopped if no improvement in 48 hours** (reassess the cause).
- A: at normal doses it does **not** cross the blood-brain barrier well (CNS depression is an overdose/child issue). C: **it is NOT indicated for antibiotic-associated diarrhoea** (risk of toxic megacolon). D: hypertension is not a contraindication.

**Q5 → A (examiner's key).** The same stem as Q4 but with different options. Here **B ("continue... more than 24 hours") is wrong** (stop at 48 h and seek review), C is wrong, D is wrong. The only statement left that is true is that loperamide **can** cause CNS depression (especially in overdose and in small children, since P-glycoprotein normally keeps it out of the brain).
- *Skill:* in "true statement" items, **change of the options changes the answer**. Always compare each option to the facts, not to the previous paper's key.

**Q6 → True.** Opioids act on mu receptors in the gut wall, slow peristalsis and raise sphincter tone, so more water is absorbed and stools harden (constipation).

**Q7 → True.** *Lactobacillus* is a probiotic: it competes with harmful bacteria for space and food and makes lactic acid, which lowers gut pH.

**Q8 → True.** Loperamide is the antimotility drug for chronic non-infective diarrhoea once infection is excluded. It is **not** for acute infective or bloody diarrhoea, antibiotic colitis, or children under 2.

**Q9 → True.** Opioid antidiarrhoeals plus alcohol add up as CNS depressants. Advise no alcohol and no driving.

*Tip:* "opioid + alcohol/sedative" is nearly always **True**.

## PART E: HOW TO ALWAYS GET THESE RIGHT
1. **"Antibiotic-associated/induced diarrhoea" → probiotic, oral rehydration; NEVER loperamide.**
2. **"Replenish flora" → Enterogermina/Lactobacillus.**
3. **Clindamycin → 50S, anaerobes, bone, toxic shock, C. difficile risk.**
4. **Loperamide:** 48-hour rule, no antibiotic diarrhoea, no dysentery, no children under 2.
5. **Handwashing with soap and water** for C. difficile.

### Memory aids
- **"Clinda = C-diff Culprit"** (clindamycin, cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones, ampicillin are the classic triggers: "**4 Cs**": Clindamycin, Cephalosporins, Ciprofloxacin (quinolones), Co-amoxiclav/aminopenicillins).
- **"Anti-motility traps the toxin."**
- **"Probiotics restore, Zinc repairs, ORS rehydrates."**

## PART G: SECTION SUMMARY
- Lincosamides (clindamycin, lincomycin): **50S, bacteriostatic**, treat **staph/strep, anaerobes, bone, toxic shock**.
- **Clindamycin → pseudomembranous colitis (C. difficile).** Stop drug, no antimotility drugs, vancomycin/fidaxomicin/metronidazole, soap-and-water handwashing.
- Mild antibiotic diarrhoea: **probiotics + ORS**.
- Loperamide: **stop at 48 h**, not for antibiotic/bloody diarrhoea, not for under 2s.
