## PART A: THEORY

### A1. Overview of the main classes (slide: Classes of Antibiotics)
Beta-lactams (**penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems, monobactams**) · **Macrolides and ketolides** · **Aminoglycosides** · **Fluoroquinolones** · **Tetracyclines and amphenicols (chloramphenicol)**. The rest of this guide takes each class in the same order as your slides.

### A2. What are penicillins?
- **Oldest class of antibiotics** (from the mould *Penicillium*).
- All **beta-lactams** (penicillins and cephalosporins) share a **beta-lactam ring**, the active part.
- **Mechanism:** they block the enzymes (penicillin-binding proteins) that build the **cell wall**. Weak wall → bacterium swells and bursts → **bactericidal** (kills). They only work on bacteria that are **actively multiplying**.
- **Uses (slide):** skin, dental, ear, respiratory, urinary infections, gonorrhoea. (Plus syphilis, rheumatic fever prevention, meningitis.)

> **Rationale for safety:** human cells have no cell wall, so penicillins are among the **safest** antibiotics, including in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Their main danger is **allergy**.

### A3. The four groups (slides: Penicillins)

| Group | Drugs (slide) | Spectrum | Key facts (for the exam) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Narrow spectrum** (mainly Gram-positive) | **Benzyl penicillin (Pen G, "X-pen")**, **Phenoxymethyl penicillin (Pen V)** | Streptococci, pneumococci, some Neisseria, *Treponema* (syphilis) | **Benzyl penicillin** is destroyed by stomach acid → **IV/IM only**. **Pen V** is acid-stable → **oral** |
| **2. Penicillinase-resistant** | **Methicillin, oxacillin, flucloxacillin** (and cloxacillin) | Staphylococci that make penicillinase | Bulky side chain **protects the beta-lactam ring** from beta-lactamase. **Not active against MRSA**. Take flucloxacillin **1 hour before food**. Rare cholestatic jaundice |
| **3. Broad spectrum** (aminopenicillins) | **Amoxicillin, ampicillin** | Gram-positive **plus** many Gram-negatives (*E. coli*, *Proteus*, *H. influenzae*) | Used for UTI, chest infection, otitis media. Destroyed by beta-lactamase. Amoxicillin better absorbed orally than ampicillin |
| **4. Augmented / potentiated** | **Augmentin (co-amoxiclav) = amoxicillin + clavulanic acid** | Broad **and** beta-lactamase-producing bacteria | Clavulanic acid blocks beta-lactamase (Section 2). Diarrhoea is common; rarely jaundice |

**Extra depot forms (tested):** **Benzathine penicillin** is a **slow-release (depot) form of benzyl penicillin**, given **deep IM only (NEVER IV)**. Used for syphilis and for preventing **rheumatic fever**. **Procaine penicillin** is a shorter-acting IM depot.

### A4. Side effects (slide)
1. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, stomach upset (common, mild).
2. **Hypersensitivity**: skin rash up to **anaphylaxis**. This is the main serious danger.
3. Extras worth knowing: **superinfection** (thrush, C. difficile), seizures with very high IV doses, rash with ampicillin in glandular fever.

### A5. Nursing care
- **Ask about allergy before every first dose**; document it; check for **cross-allergy with cephalosporins** (about 5 to 10 percent).
- Never give oral penicillin with **acidic juice** (acid destroys acid-labile ones); use **water**.
- **Observe 15 to 30 minutes** after injection; keep **adrenaline (epinephrine)** ready.
- **Anaphylaxis treatment:** **IM epinephrine** (outer mid-thigh) **immediately**. Then oxygen, fluids, laying flat with legs raised. Antihistamines and hydrocortisone are **second line** and never delay epinephrine.
- Penicillins are cleared by the kidneys; **probenecid** blocks that and prolongs their effect.

### A6. Paediatric dose arithmetic (dose is *per kg*)
**Daily dose = mg/kg/day × weight (kg). Then divide by number of doses per day.**
- Benzyl penicillin (Kenya MoH child dose): **50,000 IU/kg per dose, every 6 hours**.
- Always check dose **against a reference** and write frequency as 6-hourly, 12-hourly (not qid/bd) as recommended in Kenya's Basic Paediatric Protocols.

### A7. Kenya treatment link (pneumonia)
- **Non-severe pneumonia:** oral **amoxicillin** (high dose).
- **Severe pneumonia (children):** injectable **benzyl penicillin (or ampicillin) + gentamicin** (Basic Paediatric Protocols).
- Adults/community-acquired pneumonia: **amoxicillin** first (pneumococcus), add a **macrolide** for atypical germs (see Section 6).

## PART D: ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 → A.** Benzyl penicillin is mainly active against Gram-positive organisms, so it is **narrow spectrum**.
- B: broad-spectrum penicillins are **amoxicillin/ampicillin**.
- C: benzyl penicillin alone is not the general answer for pneumonia (it misses atypical germs and is usually paired with gentamicin in severe childhood pneumonia in Kenya).
- D: it is **easily destroyed by beta-lactamase** (this is why flucloxacillin exists). It is also destroyed by **stomach acid**, hence injection.

**Q2 → B.** Benzathine penicillin is poorly soluble, injected deep IM, and is slowly released over days to weeks.
- A false: penicillins are among the **safest drugs in pregnancy and lactation**; the only real contraindication is **penicillin allergy**.
- C false: **acid** (orange juice) reduces absorption of acid-labile penicillins; use water.
- D false: cross-allergy occurs among **beta-lactams** (penicillins, some cephalosporins). Gentamicin is an **aminoglycoside**, chemically different.

**Q3 → B. Ampicillin.** Broad spectrum (covers *E. coli*, *Proteus*), and cleared unchanged in urine.
- A, C, D are **narrow-spectrum** and are destroyed by Gram-negative beta-lactamases.
- *Real-life caution:* resistance of *E. coli* to ampicillin/amoxicillin is common, so treatment is guided by **urine culture**.

**Q4 → B. Penicillin.** Flucloxacillin is a **penicillinase-resistant penicillin** (name ends in **-cillin**).
- *Trick:* anything ending in **-cillin** is a penicillin. **-mycin/-thromycin** = macrolide/aminoglycoside. **-cycline** = tetracycline.

**Q5 → A (examiner's key). Amoxicillin, erythromycin.**
- *Logic:* community pneumonia is mostly **pneumococcus** (amoxicillin) plus **atypicals** (macrolide).
- B is the standard **injectable regimen for severe childhood pneumonia in Kenyan guidelines**, so it is also correct in that context, but the question says "first line... pneumonia" generally. If **both A and B appear**, read the stem: *general/adult* → A; *severe, child, injectable* → B.
- C: alternatives for atypicals/penicillin allergy. D: ceftriaxone and cefuroxime are **second line/reserve** (stewardship).

**Q6 → A.** Paediatric phenytoin: loading 15 to 20 mg/kg (slow IV, ECG monitoring), maintenance about 5 mg/kg/day.
- B: phenobarbitone loading is usually **15 to 20 mg/kg** (not 10).
- C: ceftriaxone is **50 to 100 mg/kg per DAY** (once daily or divided), so **100 mg/kg BD = 200 mg/kg/day is too high**.
- D: penicillin dose is about **50,000 IU/kg per dose**, so 500,000 IU/kg is **10 times too high**.
- *Tip:* spot **absurdly large numbers** (ten-fold errors) and "per day" versus "per dose" traps.

**Q7 → B. Epinephrine IM.** It reverses airway swelling (alpha-1 vasoconstriction), raises blood pressure and opens airways (beta-2). Adult 0.3 to 0.5 mg IM in the outer thigh; children 0.01 mg/kg. Repeat every 5 to 15 minutes if needed.
- A (norepinephrine IV): refractory shock only. C and D: **second line**, they do not treat shock or airway obstruction.
- *This question matters here because penicillin is the commonest drug cause of anaphylaxis.*

## PART E: HOW TO ALWAYS GET THESE RIGHT

1. **"-cillin" = penicillin = cell wall = bactericidal = beta-lactam.**
2. **Narrow vs broad:** *Benzyl/Pen V* = narrow. *Amoxicillin/ampicillin* = broad. *Co-amoxiclav* = broad + beta-lactamase proof.
3. **Staph with penicillinase → flucloxacillin/cloxacillin.** MRSA → vancomycin (Section 13).
4. **"Depot" = benzathine.** IM only.
5. In **true/false-style "true statement"** items, test every option against: *pregnancy safe? acid? cross-allergy?*
6. **Dose questions:** compute mg/kg × weight; look for decimal/ten-fold errors.
7. **Anaphylaxis = IM adrenaline first**, always.

### Memory aids
- **"Pen G is Gut-hated (acid) so Given by injection; Pen V is Very good orally."**
- **Cloxacillin = "Clox the Clever": Clever against Staph, Clear of food (empty stomach).**
- **Benzathine = Benzathine "Bad IV": never IV.**

## PART G: SECTION SUMMARY
- Penicillins: **cell wall, bactericidal, beta-lactam, safe** except for **allergy**.
- Four groups: **narrow (Pen G, Pen V), penicillinase-resistant (flucloxacillin), broad (amoxicillin, ampicillin), augmented (co-amoxiclav)**.
- Benzyl penicillin: IV/IM, acid-sensitive. Benzathine: deep IM depot, never IV.
- Side effects: GI upset; **hypersensitivity → anaphylaxis → IM epinephrine**.
- Kenya: severe child pneumonia = **benzyl penicillin (or ampicillin) + gentamicin**; non-severe = oral amoxicillin.
- Always compute doses per kg and watch for 10x errors.
