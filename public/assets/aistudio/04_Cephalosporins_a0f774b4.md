---

## PART A: THEORY

### A1. What are cephalosporins?
- **Beta-lactam antibiotics** closely related to penicillins. **Mechanism is identical:** they block cell wall synthesis, so they are **bactericidal** (slides).
- **Uses (slide):** pneumonia, strep throat, staph infections, tonsillitis, bronchitis, otitis media, skin infections, gonorrhoea, UTI, **surgical prophylaxis**; cephalexin also for **bone infections**.
- **Why different generations?** Chemists changed the molecule to widen the spectrum and make it resist beta-lactamase.

### A2. The generations (the heart of this topic)

> **Golden rule:** As generation goes **UP (1→3)**, **Gram-negative cover goes UP** and Gram-positive cover goes slightly **down**. The 4th generation has **both**. The 5th adds **MRSA**.

| Generation | Drugs on your slides | Spectrum | Key exam facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Cephradine (Velosef), **Cephalexin (Ceporex)**, **Cefadroxil (Biodroxil, Duricef)** | **Excellent Gram-positive**, poor/variable Gram-negative | Oral. Skin and soft tissue, **uncomplicated UTI**, surgical prophylaxis (cefazolin is the IV one) |
| **2nd** | **Cefaclor (Bacticlor), Cefuroxime (Zinnat)**; cefoxitin and cefotetan | Gram-positive **+ expanded Gram-negative**. Cefoxitin/cefotetan also cover *Bacteroides fragilis* (anaerobe) | Respiratory infections, sinusitis, otitis media, UTI |
| **3rd** | **Ceftriaxone (Rocephin)**, **cefotaxime**, ceftazidime (Fortum; also anti-Pseudomonas), cefixime (Suprax, oral), cefoperazone (Cefobid), cefdinir (Omnicef) | **Much expanded Gram-negative**; some have **less** Gram-positive cover | Convenient dosing, **expensive**. **Ceftriaxone + cefotaxime cross the blood-brain barrier → meningitis** |
| **4th** | **Cefepime**, cefpirome, cefluprenam, cefozopran (slide spelling "Cefasopran") | **Broad Gram-positive AND Gram-negative incl. Pseudomonas**; more **resistant to beta-lactamases** than 3rd gen; **crosses the BBB** | Hospital infections, febrile neutropenia, meningitis |
| **5th** | **Ceftaroline (Teflaro), ceftobiprole (Zeftera)** | Active against **MRSA**, penicillin-resistant pneumococci, *Pseudomonas* (ceftobiprole), enterococci (some) | Reserve drugs for resistant infections |

> **Note-check (slide detail):** The slide says "4th generation... can cross the BBB and are effective in bacterial meningitis". True, but **ceftriaxone and cefotaxime (3rd generation) are the everyday meningitis drugs**; cefepime is added when *Pseudomonas* or hospital organisms are suspected.

### A3. How cephalosporins are given
- **Oral:** cephalexin, cefadroxil, cefaclor, cefuroxime axetil, cefixime.
- **IV/IM:** ceftriaxone (**once daily**, IM with lidocaine), cefotaxime, ceftazidime, cefepime.
- **Ceftriaxone cautions:** biliary sludging; **do not mix with calcium-containing IV fluids in newborns**.

### A4. What cephalosporins do NOT cover ("LAME")
**L**isteria, **A**typicals (Chlamydia, Mycoplasma), **M**RSA (except 5th generation), **E**nterococci. This is why **pneumonia regimens add a macrolide** for atypicals.

### A5. Side effects (slide)
1. Diarrhoea, nausea, mild stomach upset.
2. **Allergy:** about **5 to 10 percent** of penicillin-allergic patients also react to cephalosporins (cross-reactivity). Avoid cephalosporins in **severe/anaphylactic** penicillin allergy.
3. **Pregnancy category B** (generally safe).
4. Extras: **C. difficile colitis**, **bleeding tendency** (some, e.g., cefoperazone, via vitamin K), alcohol reaction (cefoperazone, cefotetan).

## PART D: ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 → C.** 1st generation = **strong Gram-positive** (staph, strep) cover, modest Gram-negative.
- A: strong Gram-negative cover appears in the **3rd** generation. B: 1st-gen drugs survive **staphylococcal** penicillinase but are broken down by many Gram-negative beta-lactamases; resistance improves from the 2nd generation. D: extended Klebsiella cover = 3rd generation.

**Q2 → C (by elimination).** This item is weakly worded. First-gen drugs are narrow/moderate and are relied on for **mild to moderate infections in people with normal defences**.
- A false (Gram-negative is weak). B false (they do **not** cross the BBB; ceftriaxone/cefotaxime do). D false (cephalexin and cefadroxil are **oral**).
- *Exam skill:* when no option looks perfect, eliminate the three **definitely false** ones.

**Q3 → B.** Ceftriaxone is a **third-generation** cephalosporin: strong Gram-negative, long half-life → **once daily**, IV/IM only, crosses BBB when meninges are inflamed.
- A: **not absorbed orally**. C: Gram-positive cover is **weaker than 1st-gen**; its strength is Gram-negative. D: simple UTIs use oral drugs (nitrofurantoin, cefadroxil, cephalexin, co-trimoxazole); ceftriaxone is reserved for complicated cases.

**Q4 → B.** For meningitis, the drug must **reach the CSF**. Cefepime (4th gen) and ceftriaxone/cefotaxime (3rd gen) cross the inflamed meninges.
- C and D are **true of the class** but are *not the reason* for use in meningitis.
- *Rule:* when asked "**because**" in a use-case, choose the property that **matches the site** (brain → BBB penetration).

**Q5 → B. Cephalosporin.** Clue: **cef-/ceph-** prefix. Glycopeptide = vancomycin. Carbapenem = meropenem. Macrolide = azithromycin/erythromycin.

**Q6 → C. Cephalosporin.** Cefuroxime = **2nd-generation** cephalosporin (**cef-**). Penicillin = **-cillin**; tetracycline = **-cycline**; glycopeptide = vancomycin/teicoplanin.

**Q7 → D. Cefadroxil.** Cystitis = uncomplicated lower UTI → **oral short course** (nitrofurantoin first line where available; **oral first-generation cephalosporins** such as cefadroxil/cephalexin are good alternatives).
- A: levofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone, kept in reserve (tendon rupture, QT, resistance). B and C: **injectable**, for complicated infection or sepsis.
- *Counselling:* finish the course, drink fluids, pass urine regularly.

## PART E: HOW TO ALWAYS GET THESE RIGHT

1. **"cef-/ceph-" = cephalosporin** for class-matching questions.
2. **Generation ladder:** 1 = Gram-positive; 2 = both, plus some anaerobes (cefoxitin); 3 = Gram-negative + **CNS (meningitis)**; 4 = both + Pseudomonas + BBB; 5 = **MRSA**.
3. **Ceftriaxone facts to remember:** third generation, **IV/IM once daily**, meningitis/gonorrhoea/typhoid/severe pneumonia, **biliary sludge**, **no calcium in neonates**.
4. **Oral cephalosporin** → mild infection (cystitis, skin). **IV ceftriaxone** → severe.
5. **Allergy:** severe penicillin allergy → avoid cephalosporins (5 to 10 percent cross-reactivity).
6. **Reserve rule:** ceftriaxone/cefuroxime are **second line** for routine pneumonia and UTI (stewardship).

### Memory aids
- **"1st = 1-Gram-Positive (staph/strep), 3rd = 3-Gram-Negative."**
- **LAME** = what cephalosporins miss.
- **"Fifth fights MRSA."**
- **"Fourth is Four-ever (broad) and goes to the Brain."**

## PART G: SECTION SUMMARY
- Cephalosporins: **beta-lactams, cell wall, bactericidal**, same action as penicillins.
- **1st: Gram-positive; 2nd: wider (+ anaerobes with cefoxitin); 3rd: Gram-negative + meningitis; 4th: broadest + Pseudomonas; 5th: MRSA.**
- Ceftriaxone: IV/IM, once daily, crosses BBB, not for routine UTI.
- Side effects: GI upset, allergy (5 to 10 percent cross-reactivity), C. difficile; pregnancy category B.
- Not covered: **LAME**.
