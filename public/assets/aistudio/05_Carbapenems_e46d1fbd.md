**Lecture slides covered:** Carbapenems (two slides): description, beta-lactamase resistance, spectrum, agents, indications, IV route, side effects

## PART A: THEORY

### A1. What are carbapenems?
- A class of **beta-lactam antibiotics** with the **broadest spectrum** of all antibiotic classes.
- Mechanism: same as penicillins, they **block cell wall synthesis** (**bactericidal**).
- **Highly resistant to most beta-lactamases**, including ESBLs (extended-spectrum beta-lactamases that destroy cephalosporins).
- **Cover:** Gram-positive, Gram-negative (including ***Pseudomonas***) and **anaerobes**.
- **Do NOT cover:** **MRSA**, **atypical/intracellular bacteria** (e.g., *Chlamydia*, *Mycoplasma*) and enterococci (partly).

### A2. Agents (slide)
- **Imipenem/cilastatin (Tienem)**: imipenem is broken down by an enzyme in the kidney, so **cilastatin** is added to block that enzyme.
- **Meropenem (Meronem)**.
- Also: ertapenem (once daily, no Pseudomonas cover), doripenem.

> **Note-check (slide):** The slide's first list says "Meronem (Tienem)" as if one drug. They are **two different drugs**: **Tienem = imipenem + cilastatin; Meronem = meropenem**. The second slide lists them correctly.

### A3. Indications (slide)
1. **Hospital-acquired infections (HAIs)**, e.g., hospital-acquired pneumonia.
2. **Infected pressure sores (bedsores)**.
3. **Sepsis**, including febrile neutropenia.
4. **Anaerobic and mixed infections** (e.g., intra-abdominal sepsis).
5. Also: multi-drug resistant Gram-negatives (ESBL), meningitis (meropenem).

### A4. Route
**IV only** for imipenem and meropenem (IM ertapenem exists). Not for routine outpatient use.

### A5. Side effects (slide + extras)
| From slide | Notes |
|---|---|
| Skin rash, GI upset, diarrhoea | Common |
| Hypotension | Rapid infusion of imipenem |
| "Nephrotoxicity" | Less typical; dose must be **reduced in renal impairment** to avoid toxicity |
| **Extras (important):** **seizures**, especially **imipenem**, high dose, renal impairment | **Valproate levels fall** (do not combine). Allergy cross-reaction with penicillins is **low (under 1 percent)** but possible |

### A6. Why they are "reserve" drugs (stewardship)
Carbapenems are kept for **serious hospital-acquired and multi-drug-resistant infections**. Using them for ordinary infections breeds resistance (CRE), leaving almost no options. Nurses support stewardship by **sending cultures first**, giving doses on time and **reviewing the drug at 48 to 72 hours**.

---

## PART D: ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 → B. Carbapenem.** Meropenem, imipenem and ertapenem are carbapenems (the **-penem** ending).
- A = penicillin (-cillin). C = cephalosporin (cef-). D = vancomycin/teicoplanin.
- *Tip:* **"-penem" = carbapenem.**

**Q2 → C. Meropenem.** Broadest spectrum, stable against most beta-lactamases, **kept in reserve** for serious hospital-acquired and resistant infections.
- A and D are routine cephalosporins. B (amikacin) is a useful aminoglycoside for resistant Gram-negatives but is more oto-/nephrotoxic and is not the usual "reserve" answer.
- *Keyword shortcut:* **"reserved", "hospital-acquired", "multi-drug resistant", "broadest" → meropenem.**

---

## PART E: HOW TO ALWAYS GET THESE RIGHT
1. **"-penem" → carbapenem.**
2. **"Reserve/last-line/hospital-acquired/ESBL" → carbapenem.**
3. Carbapenem **does not cover MRSA**: that is vancomycin/ceftaroline.
4. **IV only**, reduce dose in renal impairment, **watch for seizures** (imipenem).

### Memory aids
- **"Carbapenems: the Big Gun in the hospital Bunker."**
- **"Meropenem = Most Everything, Reserve Only"** (broadest, reserve).
- **"Imipenem needs a bodyguard (cilastatin) in the kidney."**

## PART G: SECTION SUMMARY
- Carbapenems = **broadest beta-lactams**, IV, **resistant to beta-lactamases**, cover Gram-positive, Gram-negative and anaerobes.
- Agents: **meropenem, imipenem/cilastatin**.
- Uses: HAIs, sepsis, pressure sores, mixed anaerobic infections.
- Side effects: rash, GI upset, hypotension, **seizures**; adjust dose in renal impairment.
- **Reserve drug**: stewardship matters.
