**Lecture slides covered:** Aminoglycosides (mechanism, spectrum, synergy, route) → List of drugs → Adverse effects (ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity)

---

## PART A: THEORY

### A1. What are aminoglycosides?
- **Mechanism (slide):** bind the **30S ribosomal subunit** → the ribosome **misreads** the genetic code → faulty proteins → bacterium dies. They are **bactericidal** (unusual for protein synthesis inhibitors).
- **Spectrum (slide):** mainly **Gram-negative** bacteria (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Pseudomonas*, *Proteus*). Weak against Gram-positives and **anaerobes** (they need oxygen to get into the cell). Streptomycin also acts on **TB**.
- **Synergy (slide):** often combined with **penicillins/beta-lactams** (cell wall damage helps the drug get in).
- **Route (slide):** **IV or IM**. They are **not absorbed from the gut** (the slide says "destroyed by acids"; the true reason is that the molecules are too big and charged to cross the gut wall). **Oral neomycin** is used only for **bowel preparation** and **hepatic encephalopathy** because it stays in the gut.

### A2. Drugs (slide)
**Gentamicin**, **streptomycin**, **amikacin**, **kanamycin**, **neomycin**, **tobramycin**.
Name clue: **"-mycin" with an "-my-"** (gentaMYcin, kanaMYcin...), plus **-micin/-kacin** names.

### A3. Adverse effects (slide): the two famous ones
| Toxicity | What happens | Why (rationale) | Nursing action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ototoxicity** | Tinnitus, hearing loss, **vertigo/imbalance** (vestibular). Can be **permanent** | Drug builds up in the **inner-ear fluid** and damages hair cells | Ask about hearing/dizziness daily; baseline and follow-up audiometry in long courses; avoid **loop diuretics (furosemide)** |
| **Nephrotoxicity** | Rising creatinine, **oliguria/anuria** (acute tubular necrosis). Usually **reversible** | Drug concentrates in **kidney tubule cells** | Check **urea, creatinine, urine output**; keep well **hydrated**; **reduce dose** in renal impairment; avoid other nephrotoxic drugs (vancomycin, NSAIDs, amphotericin) |

**Other points:** **neuromuscular blockade** (rapid injection, myasthenia); narrow therapeutic index → check **peak and trough levels**; **fetal ototoxicity (avoid in pregnancy**, especially streptomycin).
**Risk factors:** **old age**, dehydration, kidney disease, high dose, long courses (>7 to 10 days), other toxic drugs.

### A4. Dosing and calculations (Kenya)
- Older teaching: **3 mg/kg/day in 2 to 3 divided doses**.
- Current practice (Kenya paediatric trial and protocols): **once daily** higher-dose gentamicin (about **5 to 7.5 mg/kg once daily**), because aminoglycosides kill by **peak concentration** and toxicity is lower with a **drug-free trough**.
- The **exam stem will tell you the plan**; follow the stem.
- **Worked example (Apr 2025 Q8):** child 10 kg, plan 3 mg/kg/day for 5 days → 30 mg/day → **BD = 15 mg every 12 hours IV for 5 days**.

### A5. Other related drug: capreomycin
Not an aminoglycoside (it is a **polypeptide** second-line anti-TB injectable) but has the **same toxicities** (oto + nephrotoxic).

## PART D: ANSWERS AND EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 → A. Aminoglycosides.** Gentamicin binds 30S, bactericidal, mainly Gram-negative, IV/IM, oto + nephrotoxic. B = azithromycin; C = ciprofloxacin; D = vancomycin.

**Q2 → B. Aminoglycoside.** (same). A = ciprofloxacin; C = amoxicillin/flucloxacillin; D = erythromycin/clarithromycin.

**Q3 → C. Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity.** Nephrotoxicity = tubule damage; ototoxicity = inner-ear damage.
- A: ocular toxicity belongs to **ethambutol**; black stool = iron/bismuth. B: aminoglycosides do **not** damage the liver. D: incomplete and rash is not characteristic.

**Q4 → C.** Neomycin is also an aminoglycoside. Although poorly absorbed, enough can be absorbed through **ulcerated bowel, kidney impairment or large raw skin areas** to cause toxicity.

**Q5 → C. Hearing loss, anuria.** Kidney function **falls with age**, so an older patient clears gentamicin slowly, builds up levels and risks **acute kidney injury (anuria/oliguria)** and **deafness**.
- A: black stools are iron/bismuth. B: no liver toxicity. D: minor effects.
- *Tip:* "elderly + aminoglycoside" → **kidney and ear**.

**Q6 → A. IV 15mg BD for 5/7.**
1. 3 mg/kg/day × 10 kg = **30 mg per day**.
2. **BD = twice daily** → 30 ÷ 2 = **15 mg per dose**.
3. 5/7 = **5 days**.
4. **Route:** gentamicin is **not absorbed orally** → **IV (or IM)**, so B and C (P.O.) are wrong.
5. D (30 mg BD) = 60 mg/day = **double** the plan.
- *Always do:* (daily dose) → (÷ doses per day) → (check route).

**Q7 → B. Tobramycin (examiner's key).** Among the listed drugs, tobramycin has the **lowest combined** oto- and nephrotoxicity. Gentamicin is more nephrotoxic. Kanamycin is highly ototoxic. Streptomycin is **least nephrotoxic** but **strongly vestibulotoxic** (dizziness, imbalance), so it fails "least ototoxic".
- *Note-check:* the stem asks for *both* low nephrotoxicity **and** low ototoxicity; that is why streptomycin is not accepted.

**Q8 → A. Capreomycin.** Second-line injectable for **MDR-TB**; **oto + nephrotoxic**, like aminoglycosides (monitor kidney function, K+, Mg2+, hearing).
- B ethambutol → **optic neuritis**. C isoniazid → **peripheral neuropathy, hepatitis**. D pyrazinamide → **hepatotoxicity, hyperuricaemia**.

**Q9 → B. ACE inhibitors, Warfarin.** Both are teratogenic/fetotoxic.
- C is wrong as a *pair* because **antacids** are safe, although streptomycin **does** harm the fetal ear (deafness). A: ceftriaxone is safe. D: paracetamol and several antimalarials (artemether-lumefantrine in later pregnancy, quinine) are used.
- *Strategy:* a pair is only correct if **both** drugs are harmful.

---

## PART E: HOW TO ALWAYS GET THESE RIGHT
1. **Aminoglycoside = Ears + Kidneys.** If an option pair has "nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity" it wins. **Hepatotoxicity is a decoy.**
2. **Elderly, dehydrated, kidney disease → worse toxicity.**
3. **Route = IV/IM only** (never "P.O.", except neomycin for gut decontamination).
4. **Dose maths:** per-day dose → divide by frequency (BD = ÷2, OD = ÷1, TDS = ÷3).
5. **Pair-type options:** both drugs must satisfy the statement.
6. **Ethambutol = eyes, isoniazid = nerves, pyrazinamide = liver/gout, rifampicin = orange.**

### Memory aids
- **"Gentamicin = Gets To Ears and Nephrons."**
- **Aminoglycosides: "Never Give Orally, Never Give Fast"** (NGO: nephro, oto; fast injection → neuromuscular block).
- **"Mycin with a Y is a Yikes for ears and kidneys."**
- **Toxicity ladder:** Neomycin (most ototoxic and nephrotoxic, hence topical/oral only) > Kanamycin/Amikacin > Gentamicin > Tobramycin; **Streptomycin = vestibular**.

## PART G: SECTION SUMMARY
- Aminoglycosides: **30S, bactericidal, Gram-negative, IV/IM, synergy with beta-lactams**.
- Drugs: **gentamicin, streptomycin, amikacin, kanamycin, neomycin, tobramycin**.
- **Adverse: ototoxicity + nephrotoxicity**; danger in the elderly, dehydration, renal disease.
- Monitor creatinine, urine output, hearing, levels; keep hydrated; avoid furosemide.
- **Dose = mg/kg/day ÷ number of doses; give IV/IM.**
- Capreomycin (TB) shares the toxicity; streptomycin harms the fetal ear.
